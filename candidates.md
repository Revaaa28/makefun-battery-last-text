# Manual ranking (criteria in brief order + pairwise vs strongest imagined rivals)

Rival shapes I expect in the field: (a) plain sad goodbye ("goodbye, I loved you, 1%"),
(b) "you left me at 2% all day" complaint with no turn, (c) "don't forget to charge me"
nag, (d) mid-sentence cut-off gag. All of (a)–(c) lose on criterion 2 (no final-word
turn); (d) is clever once but reads as a formatting trick, weak on criterion 3 (not
something a person would type).

- #6 vs #4: both spiteful, both land the turn in the final words. #6 wins: "taking
  your alarms with me" is a concrete revenge with a consequence for the owner, funnier
  and more specific than "i hope your cable frays", and "you only notice me now? cute."
  is a stronger voice than "like a dog". #6 > #4.
- #6 vs #3: #3's jealousy setup (tablet/earbuds/watch) is the best setup in the set —
  relatable, specific, voice — but its payoff "i'm done" is generic and spends the
  final words on nothing. #6's payoff is the joke. #6 > #3 on criterion 2.
- #14 vs #6: #14 keeps #6's payoff and replaces its setup with #3's jealousy setup,
  which earns the spite more (the charger didn't forget — it chose other devices).
  Every clause earns its place; no filler. #14 > #6.
- #14 vs #13: #13's loose-cable reveal is a real turn, but it ends on a flat "bye" —
  the punchline lands mid-text and the final words are dead air. #14 ends ON the
  punchline. #14 > #13 on criterion 2.
- #14 vs #11: #11's "1% immortal" twist is the best second-read joke in the set, but
  it has no addressee, no spite, no text-message voice — it reads as a caption, not a
  text to the charger, and loses on criteria 1 and 3. #14 > #11.
- #14 vs #2/#9: same jealousy family, but #2/#9 end on "traitor / i'm out" — a label,
  not a joke. #14's final words deliver the consequence. #14 > #2, #9.
- #14 vs #5: #5's "oh wait. that's you" is cute but convoluted (the battery texting
  the charger to "tell the charger" is muddled) and its final word is "bye". #14 is
  clean, on-subject, and ends on the punchline. #14 > #5.
- #1, #7, #8, #10, #12: all land somewhere but each has a flat or muddled ending
  ("don't text back", "find another battery", "not the vape", "slowpoke", "i told the
  tablet everything") — none beats #14's final-word payoff.

WINNER: #14 — "all day i watched you charge the tablet, the earbuds, even the watch.
i'm at 1% and this is my last text. going to 0% now, and i'm taking your alarms with me."
Why it wins: criterion 1 — unmistakably a last text to the charger, on-subject;
criterion 2 — the turn pays off in the very last words (revenge: the alarms die with
it); criterion 3 — lowercase texting voice, jealous and spiteful, exactly how a person
texts; criterion 4 — every clause earns the punchline, no filler; criterion 5 — the
angle is jealousy + revenge, not "I'm dying", and it stays funny on a second read
(the charger chose the watch over the phone, so the battery takes the alarms).
